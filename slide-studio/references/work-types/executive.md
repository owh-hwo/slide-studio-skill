# Work type: executive / board / steering-committee presentation

Executives read titles, glance at one visual, and decide. Lead with the answer; every slide
earns its place by supporting the decision you are asking for.

## Extra interview questions

- The ask: what exactly must be decided or approved, by whom, by when? (one sentence)
- What do they already know; what will they challenge? (prepare an appendix slide per likely
  challenge)
- Pre-read or live? If pre-read, leads may be fuller; the deck must stand alone.
- Numbers: source system and cut-off date for every figure; currency and units.
- Sensitivities: names of people, vendors, internal disagreements.

## Storyline skeleton (Pyramid principle: answer first)

| Part | Slides | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Cover: title = the answer; 4 messages = the supporting points | 1 | cover |
| Executive summary: situation, complication, answer, the ask | 1 | table.big or principles |
| One section per supporting point: 1 to 3 slides each, one chart or table per slide | 4 to 9 | kpis, table, diagram, compare |
| Options with a recommendation (if a decision) | 1 to 2 | compare, table.price |
| The ask / decision slide | 1 | section (closing) or card.brand |
| Appendix (after the closing slide): backup data, methodology | any | table |

## Sizing

- 10 to 15 main slides for a 30 to 45 minute slot; 2 to 3 minutes per slide.
- If a slide needs more than one minute of explanation to be understood, split it.

## Style

- Titles carry numbers when they can ("Costs 0.2 M baht above plan, inside the reserve").
- KPI tiles: at most 4 per slide; one `.focus` tile for the number the slide is about.
- Tables: at most 6 rows on a main slide; detail goes to the appendix.
- Notes: short talking points plus the expected question and the answer.

## Pitfalls

- Building up to the conclusion on the last slide. Put it on the cover.
- Methodology and history before the answer.
- Unsourced numbers. Every number has a source and date in the notes or a footnote.
