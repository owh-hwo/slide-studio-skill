# Work type: proposal / pre-sales pitch

The client must see their own problem, believe the approach fits, and know what happens next.
Write in the client's words, not the vendor's product vocabulary.

## Extra interview questions

- The client: industry, size, what triggered the project, who decides, who influences.
- Their stated requirements (TOR, RFP, meeting notes): read them first.
- Scope: what is in, what is explicitly out; phases.
- Commercials: may prices appear? Which model (fixed, T&M, subscription)? Validity date.
- Evidence: references, case studies, certifications that may be named (with permission).
- Competitors: never named unless the user insists.
- Next step you want (workshop, demo, signature) and its date.

## Storyline skeleton

| Part | Slides | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Cover: the outcome for the client, not "Proposal for X" | 1 | cover |
| Their situation and objectives, in their words | 1 to 2 | principles, table |
| What success looks like (measurable) | 1 | kpis or objs |
| Approach / solution overview | 1 to 3 | diagram, phases |
| Scope: in / out | 1 | compare or table with band rows |
| Plan and phases, with what the client provides | 1 to 2 | phases, table |
| Team and governance | 1 | table, diagram (org chart) |
| Why us: evidence only (references, numbers) | 1 | cases or table |
| Commercials | 1 | table.price |
| Next steps with dates and owners | 1 | steps or ck |
| Appendix: details, assumptions, terms | any | table |

## Sizing

12 to 20 slides; leave-behind version may add a handout (`--handout`).

## Pitfalls

- Feature lists. Map each capability to a stated client need or drop it.
- Claims without proof ("best in class"). Use a reference, a number, or nothing.
- Prices without assumptions and validity date.
